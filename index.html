01Approach

Extreme environments reward those who understand the risks and know how to respond.

Weather forecasts in the polar regions are uncertain, conditions can change rapidly, and a wrong forecast interpretation can be costly. In the New Arctic, sea-ice intelligence is what can make operations both safe and fuel efficient.

We plan to take advantage of the new and unique capabilities of frontier AI systems, Starlink-enabled polar communications and our deep domain expertise, to build the best possible sea-ice intelligence layer for enhanced polar operations and risk management.

02Capabilities

Predict

Forecast future sea ice conditions, from hours to months ahead. Reconstruct prior conditions (hindcasts) to better understand what was possible. Fuse data from satellite observations, in-situ sensors, weather/climate models and machine learning.

Assess

Clearly quantify forecast uncertainty and associated risk impacts, moving from deterministic to Bayesian/probabilistic analysis. Use hindcasts to enhance our understanding of past performance and improve future forecasts.

Operate

Turn sea ice forecasts into decision-ready intelligence that keeps people, vessels and missions safe, delivered rapidly and seamlessly into the systems you already use.

03Fram

Fram, the first of many 82 NORTH intelligence layers.

Vessel-specific ice intelligence, delivered into the workflows operators and navigation companies already use. As Arctic sea ice becomes more fragmented and mobile, Fram clearly communicates the risks and opportunities for a given vessel class, now and in the weeks and months ahead.

Hours to days

Nowcast

Live ice risk for vessels underway: current conditions, near-term change, and which routes are open for a given vessel class.

Weeks to months

Seasonal

Probabilistic outlooks of ice conditions and access windows to support scheduling, chartering and mission planning.

Past seasons

Hindcast

Look back at routes and decisions: what the ice did, which alternatives existed, and what they meant for fuel, cost and risk.

Sea ice is our first focus, but our long-term ambition is broader operational intelligence: helping people make better decisions in Earth's most uncertain environments.

04Nansen

Inspired by a bet on the ice.

In 1893, Fridtjof Nansen decided that instead of fighting the Arctic pack, he would let his ship freeze into it. He had studied how debris from a wreck off Siberia had drifted across the Arctic Ocean to Greenland, and bet that the ice itself could carry his ship across the Arctic.

He had Fram built for that purpose. Her rounded hull was designed so that the pressure of the ice would lift her up rather than crush her. She drifted for three years, reached higher latitudes than any ship before her, and came home intact.

That is the spirit behind 82 NORTH: understand the environment deeply enough that uncertainty becomes something you can work with, and put that understanding to work for the people who depend on it.
